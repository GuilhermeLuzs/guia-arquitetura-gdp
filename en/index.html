Software Architecture Web Guide

This site is an academic project for the Project Management course unit at SENAI Félix Guisard School, in Taubaté, Brazil. In it, the team analyzes three posts from Martin Fowler's blog about software architecture and shows how each technical decision affects the management of a project. For each post, the team explains the core idea, points out the advantages and disadvantages, and relates the concept to the project's schedule, cost, team and risks.

The three posts and the life cycle of a system

The team chose three posts that, together, represent the stages in the life of a system, from its first version to the moment it grows old and needs to be modernized. This way, each post answers a question that project management faces at a different moment.

How to start? In Monolith First, Martin Fowler discusses whether a new system should be born as a monolith or already split into microservices. Starting with a monolith makes it possible to validate the product faster and to see over time where it makes sense to split, but the author himself acknowledges that the question remains open.

How to organize it internally? As the system grows, Presentation Domain Data Layering deals with the separation into presentation, domain and data, so that each part can be developed, reused and changed without redoing the others. In more complex systems, these layers can be further divided into smaller parts.

What to do when it grows old? Over the years, a system can become a legacy that is expensive and hard to change. Patterns of Legacy Displacement shows how to replace it gradually, with the new system running alongside the old one, instead of betting on a complete replacement all at once.

In all these stages, architecture decisions directly affect the project's schedule, cost, team and risks.
